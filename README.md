# Colonial Pipeline Ransomware Attack: Research Paper

A course research paper analyzing the May 2021 Colonial Pipeline ransomware attack through three foundational cybersecurity lenses: the CIA triad and access control; identification, authentication, and authorization; and the human element of security (economics, psychology, and decision-making).

**Course:** CYB-7153-01, Foundations of Cyber Security, The University of Tulsa

## Summary

On May 7, 2021, Colonial Pipeline shut down a system that carries roughly half of the East Coast's fuel after discovering DarkSide ransomware on its IT network. The attackers got in through a legacy VPN account that was never disabled and had no multi-factor authentication. The paper argues that the incident was a chain of small failures rather than one large technical flaw: a cheap missing control, organizational habits that let it persist, and an incentive structure in which most of the cost fell on parties other than the company.

## Paper structure

1. Introduction
2. Background: the company, its IT/OT systems, the attackers, and the regulatory context
3. Technical Analysis: initial access, attack progression, CIA triad impact, missing controls
4. Human Factors: organizational error, psychology, effects on people and society
5. Economic Impact: direct and indirect costs, externalities
6. Legal and Ethical Considerations: applicable law, policy response, ethical questions
7. Lessons Learned, mapped to the three course modules
8. Conclusion
